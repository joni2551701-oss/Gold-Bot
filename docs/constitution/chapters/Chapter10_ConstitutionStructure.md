## Executive Summary

Chapter 10 documents the **structure of the Constitution itself** — how it is
organized, how a chapter is shaped, how packages are standardized, and how the
chaptered edition relates to the in-force Articles-based constitution. A
constitution that describes its own structure is easier to extend consistently
over the 40-chapter horizon and beyond.

## 1. Purpose of the Structure

The Constitution is structured so that it can be **extended routinely and amended
rarely**. A predictable structure lets each new chapter be authored, reviewed, and
consolidated the same way, keeping the growing document coherent.

## 2. Editions and Their Relationship

Two editions coexist. The **in-force Articles-based constitution**
([`CONSTITUTION.md`](../CONSTITUTION.md), [`ARTICLES.md`](../ARTICLES.md),
[`AMENDMENTS.md`](../AMENDMENTS.md)) is the operative governance in effect today.
The **chaptered edition** (this GB-CONST-001…040 series) is the enterprise-format
Constitution v1.0 that **extends and consolidates** that governance in narrative
chapters. The chaptered edition does not silently override the Articles; how the
two ultimately reconcile — whether the chaptered edition supersedes or wraps the
Articles at v1.0 — is a Director-level ruling recorded when the edition is
completed. Until then, where a rule already exists in the Articles or a policy,
the chaptered edition restates its intent and points to it as operative.

## 3. Blocks and Chapters

The chaptered edition is organized into thematic blocks:

- **Foundational block (Chapters 01–07):** Vision, Mission, Values, Core
  Principles, Long-Term Goals, Scope, Non-Goals and Terminology.
- **Governance block (Chapters 08–17):** Governance and Architecture Philosophy,
  Constitution Structure, the roles (Director, Worker, Reviewer), Quality
  Assurance, Decision Process, Documentation Governance, and Version Strategy.
- **Later blocks (Chapter 18 onward):** domain strategies and standards, added as
  the edition grows.

## 4. Chapter Anatomy

Each chapter follows one shape: a header stating package, status, block, and
continuity; an **Executive Summary**; a **chapter Table of Contents**; a set of
numbered sections (typically eight); and a closing marker. Governance chapters also
cite the **operative source** they consolidate. This uniform anatomy is what lets
chapters be reviewed against a single checklist.

## 5. Package Standard

Each chapter is delivered from a standardized work package containing: `01_TASK`,
`02_REQUIREMENTS`, `03_ACCEPTANCE_CRITERIA`, `04_STYLE_GUIDE`,
`05_WORKER_RESPONSE_TEMPLATE`, `06_REVIEW_CHECKLIST`, `07_STATUS`, and
`08_CHANGELOG`. The standard makes every chapter deterministic to execute and
review, and it is applied uniformly across single packages and batches.

## 6. Extension versus Amendment

The Constitution grows primarily by **extension** — adding a new chapter or a new
work package — and only exceptionally by **amendment** of what already stands. An
amendment to an approved chapter is a recorded, Director-level decision; routine
growth adds material rather than rewriting it. This preserves the stability the
governing documents are meant to have.

## 7. Sources of Truth (Compose-and-Link)

The Constitution follows a compose-and-link discipline: each governance domain has
a **single source of truth**, and other documents reference it rather than
restating it. A governance chapter that overlaps an existing policy or standard
states the constitutional principle and links the operative document, so rules are
never duplicated and cannot drift apart.

## 8. Structural Integrity

The structure is kept sound by consistency: reserved terms carry one meaning
(Chapter 07), each chapter fits the anatomy above, each governance domain has one
source of truth, and consolidation decisions are recorded. A chapter that
duplicates a rule, redefines a reserved term, or contradicts an approved chapter
fails structural review and is corrected before acceptance.
