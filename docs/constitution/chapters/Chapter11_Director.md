## Executive Summary

Chapter 11 defines the **Director** role — the authority that sets direction,
reviews work, and authorizes change. The Director gives the system its intent and
guards its boundaries, but the Director's authority is itself bounded by the
Constitution: it cannot be used to weaken the safety guarantees or the gateway
boundary. This chapter states the role's authority, responsibilities, and limits;
the operative rules live in the Director Policy.

## 1. Role Statement

The Director is the role that **sets direction and authorizes change**. The
Director decides what is built and in what order, reviews completed work, and
grants the authorization required for a change to take effect.

## 2. Authority

The Director holds authority to: define and prioritize work packages; approve or
reject designs and deliverables; authorize merges to the protected main line; move
the scope boundary through a recorded decision; and rule on questions escalated by
the Worker. This authority is the mechanism by which intent enters the system.

## 3. Responsibilities

The Director is responsible for keeping direction consistent with the vision,
mission, and principles (Chapters 01–04); for reviewing work against the standards;
for making rulings when the Worker escalates; and for ensuring that consequential
decisions are recorded. The Director owns the coherence of the roadmap.

## 4. Review and Approval

No consequential change takes effect without the Director's review and
authorization. The Director reviews designs before implementation and deliverables
before merge, applying the review checklist and the safety gates. Approval is
explicit and recorded; absence of objection is not approval.

## 5. Direction and Prioritization

The Director sets and re-orders the roadmap, choosing which work packages proceed
and when. Prioritization is a Director prerogative, exercised within the scope and
non-goals of Chapters 06–07 — direction may reorder work, but it does not move the
system toward a non-goal without a recorded scope decision.

## 6. Limits of Authority

The Director's authority is bounded by the Constitution. It **cannot** be used to
bypass the risk controls, to let the advisory intelligence execute, to introduce a
second entry point to the Core, or to place client logic in the Core. These
boundaries are not the Director's to trade away; they are properties of the system
the Director exists to protect.

## 7. Interaction with the Worker

The Director issues direction; the Worker executes it. The Director answers the
Worker's escalated questions and reviews the Worker's deliverables. The two roles
are deliberately separate (Chapter 08): the Director does not perform the Worker's
execution, and the Worker does not assume the Director's authority.

## 8. Accountability

The Director is accountable for keeping authority within the Constitution and for
the integrity of the decisions made. Because direction and approvals are recorded,
the Director's choices remain part of the audit trail and can be examined after the
fact.
