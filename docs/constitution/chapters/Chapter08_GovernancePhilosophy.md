## Executive Summary

Chapters 01–07 established what GoldBot is; Chapter 08 opens the governance block
by stating **how GoldBot is governed** — the philosophy behind its rules, roles,
and decisions. Governance in GoldBot exists to protect the foundation, the user,
and the audit trail; it is a means of keeping the vision intact, not an end in
itself. This chapter states the convictions that later governance chapters
(Structure, Director, Worker, Reviewer, Quality Assurance, Decision Process,
Documentation, Version) then make concrete.

## 1. Governance Statement

GoldBot is governed to keep its **foundation stable, its user protected, and its
history auditable**. Governance is the discipline by which direction is set,
decisions are made and recorded, and changes are reviewed before they take
effect. It applies equally to every work package and every surface.

## 2. Purpose of Governance

Governance exists to ensure that the commitments of Chapters 01–03 survive
contact with day-to-day work: that speed never quietly overrides foundation
integrity, that boundaries are never traded for convenience, and that every
consequential action leaves a record. Governance is protection, expressed as
process.

## 3. Principles of Governance

- **Foundation and safety first.** Governance never authorizes a change that
  weakens the Core boundary or the safety guarantees.
- **Recorded over remembered.** Consequential direction and decisions are written
  down, not held informally.
- **Review before effect.** Change is reviewed before it is merged.
- **Single source of truth.** Each governance domain has one authoritative source;
  rules are not duplicated across documents.
- **Reuse before creation.** Governance prefers extending an existing rule or
  document to inventing a parallel one.

## 4. Authority and Accountability

Authority in GoldBot is bounded and accountable. Whoever holds authority to set
direction or approve change is also accountable for keeping that authority within
the Constitution — no role may authorize a bypass of the safety guarantees or the
gateway boundary. Authority is paired with a recorded trail, so that decisions can
be examined after the fact.

## 5. Separation of Roles

Governance separates the roles that set direction, execute work, and review it, so
that no single role both authors and unilaterally approves a consequential change.
The specific roles — Director, Worker, Reviewer — and their limits are defined in
Chapters 11–13; the philosophy here is that their separation is deliberate and
protective.

## 6. No Silent Decisions

A consequential choice that cannot be resolved from the brief is escalated for a
ruling, never made quietly. Silence is not consent, and an undocumented decision
is treated as a governance defect. This principle, first stated among the decision
values of Chapter 03, is foundational to how governance operates.

## 7. Governance and Safety

Governance and Trading Safety are aligned, not in tension: the purpose of
governance is, above all, to keep the safety guarantees permanent. No governance
process, however expedient, may be used to weaken the rule that risk controls are
never bypassed or that advisory intelligence never executes.

## 8. Long-Term Governance

Over the long horizon, governance is meant to remain **stable and light**: the
governing documents are amended rarely and extended routinely, roles endure, and
the process for delivering a work package stays recognizable from one year to the
next. Institutional memory — decisions, lessons, and standards — is preserved so
the system does not relearn what it has already settled.
