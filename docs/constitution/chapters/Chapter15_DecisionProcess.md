## Executive Summary

Chapter 15 defines the **decision process** — how consequential choices are made,
escalated, and recorded. GoldBot's governing conviction is that no consequential
decision is silent: each is either resolvable from an authorized brief or escalated
for a ruling, and each is recorded so it can be examined later. This chapter states
the process; the decision records live in the referenced sources.

## 1. Decision Statement

GoldBot makes consequential decisions **deliberately and on the record**. A
decision that affects direction, architecture, scope, or safety is made by the
authorized role, with its rationale captured.

## 2. Kinds of Decisions

Decisions are categorized so they can be tracked: **direction and governance
decisions** (set by the Director), **architecture and design decisions** (recorded
as decision-log entries or architecture decision records), and **acceptance
decisions** (the recorded acceptance of a completed work package). Each kind has a
place in the record; none is made informally.

## 3. No Silent Decisions

The central rule, carried from Chapters 03 and 08: a consequential choice that
cannot be resolved from the brief is escalated, not made quietly. An undocumented
consequential decision is treated as a defect, because a decision no one can trace
cannot be reviewed or relied upon.

## 4. Escalation

When execution meets an ambiguity that changes what is built, the Worker escalates
it to the Director with enough context to rule — the assumptions, the alternatives,
and the recommendation. Escalation is the normal, expected response to genuine
ambiguity, not a failure.

## 5. Recording Decisions

A consequential decision is recorded with its rationale and its authorization, in
the single decision record for its kind. Recording is what turns a choice into part
of the institutional memory, so that later work can build on settled decisions
rather than reopening them.

## 6. Reversibility

Where outcomes are uncertain, the more reversible option is preferred, and
irreversible or outward-facing actions are confirmed before they are taken. A
decision's reversibility is weighed as part of making it, not discovered
afterward.

## 7. Decisions and Safety

No decision process may be used to weaken the safety guarantees. A choice that
would bypass the risk controls, let the advisory intelligence execute, or breach
the gateway boundary is out of bounds regardless of who proposes it or how it is
recorded. Safety is not a decidable trade-off.

## 8. Traceability

Because decisions are recorded, the system's direction is traceable: any
consequential outcome can be followed back to the decision, the rationale, and the
authorization behind it. This traceability is a core part of the audit trail the
Constitution requires.
